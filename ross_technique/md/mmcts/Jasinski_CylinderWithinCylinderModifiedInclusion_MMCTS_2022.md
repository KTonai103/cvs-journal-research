We illustrate a procedure for implanting a modified stentless cylinder in a patient with bicuspid aortic valve disease with an aortic aneurysm. The modification is based on a unique technique of continuous suturing that mimics reimplantation or the David operation. This technical variant can be applied to auto- and heterografts.

The patient is a 50-year-old man with a bicuspid aortic valve type I showing severe stenosis and valve calcifications, with a modest concomitant aortic annulus and root and aorta enlargement of 29 mm, 45 mm and 52 mm, respectively.

The outflow suture line is created with 5-0 continuous Prolene suture to secure the cylinder by reimplanting it from the inside to the outside of the aortic root and all the way back. This line begins at the nadirs and runs on both sides from the inside to the outside of each of the two coronary sinuses and back; it resembles the reimplantation technique used in the David operation. The noncoronary sinus forms a neo-aortic root. The technique offers the proven benefits of stabilization of the neo-aortic root within the native root, which is essential for both stentless autograft implants and bicuspid root pathology.

The feasibility and advantages of implanting a stentless graft in a dysfunctional bicuspid aortic valve (BAV) with associated aortic root enlargement have been demonstrated. This procedure has recently become even more relevant as more stentless autograft implants have been recommended for patients below 50 years of age. There is, however, a significant rate of failure related to neo-aortic root enlargement after the full-root implant of a stentless autograft has been demonstrated \[1\]. The operation performed for patients with a BAV seems to be even less durable, which may partly be due to connective tissue disorders being an inherent feature of a BAV. Annular progressive dilatation as a consequence of annulo-aortic ectasia may also affect the long-term stability and progression of aortic regurgitation \[2\]. On the other hand, recent data support stentless auto- and heterografts as preferable choices for endocarditis, which will remain a surgical disease \[3\].

Reimplanting a cylinder-within-a-cylinder, the modified inclusion technique presented here, has been developed to make the technique simpler and more reproducible, while successfully addressing issues of annular and root stabilization at the same time. The modification is based on the unique technique of a second outflow line of continuous suturing during the implant process. It mimics the fundamental elements of a modified reimplantation previously presented by the author \[4\]. The haemodynamic advantage is a consequence of maintaining a deep U-shaped distal line rather than a V-shaped line, which has been proven superior during a valve-sparing reimplantation procedure. Indeed, it has recently been shown that the detailed in vitro haemodynamics are comparable to or more favourable than valve-sparing operations, depending upon the technical variant, as presented during the 36th Annual EACTS annual meeting in 2022. Implanting an ascending aorta prosthesis further stabilizes the sinotubular junction (STJ) and completes a distal line above the coronaries in a trapdoor fashion.

The patient is a 50-year-old man with BAV type I according to the Sievers classification. The valve shows severe stenosis with a mean gradient of 60 mmHg. There is moderate root enlargement, up to 45 mm, and the aorta has a diameter of 51 mm.

- 1 - Stentless mini-root cylinder-within-a-cylinder reimplantation (0:12)
	We present our stentless mini-root cylinder-within-a-cylinder reimplantation technique.
- 2 - Assessment of the aortic valve and root (0:37)
	We perform a careful and complete dissection of the aortic root from the pulmonary artery in a Ross plane. Cross-clamping followed by a transverse aortotomy is performed. We make a provisional assessment of the anatomy of the aortic valve. The echocardiographic findings of a BAV type I with left-right fusion and severe calcifications are confirmed. We then do a thorough excision and debridement step.
	We use Hegar and Freestyle sizers to decide on the correct diameter for the heterograft and the ascending aorta prosthesis. This measurement corresponds to the proper prosthesis diameter.
- 3 - First-line aortic root sutures and prosthesis management (1:07)
	The stentless graft prosthesis is secured with the first line of non-pledgeted sutures intra-annularly. The first line of 18 single interrupted non-pledgeted, 2-0 Ethibond stitches is located horizontally at the ventriculo-aortic junction. Suturing begins with the non-left commissures at the bottom of the fibrotic subcommissural triangle. The left sinus is secured in a clockwise manner followed by deep sutures at the commissures between the left and right sinuses. The right sinus is secured 5 mm below the upper ventriculo-aortic junction in order to maintain a horizontal plane at the interventricular septum followed by the non-right commissure. Attention is paid to the conduction system below the non-right subcommissural triangle, which is well defined after pulling the sutures apart to identify the membranous septum. If necessary, the graft is trimmed at this point. Then, in a counterclockwise manner, single sutures are placed at a slightly higher level at the non-coronary sinus to compensate for root asymmetry and a slight tilt.
- 4 - Implanting a miniroot (3:10)
	After trimming the coronary sinuses, a miniroot is established. Then the resulting cylinder is pulled down under continuous perpendicular traction, and the sutures are tied down. We assess the implanted cylinder at this stage to place the right-left commissures at the proper level. A cylinder-within-a-cylinder is formed and assessed for proper anatomical position.
- 5 - Implanting the aortic valve (3:48)
	The second outflow line is created with 5-0 continuous Prolene suture, handled from the inside the entire way. This line begins below the right coronary ostium and runs continuously towards the non-right commissures. As early as possible, it goes outside the root and then from outside to inside, to reimplant this cylinder into the native root. After completion, the line is secured by locking with another 5-0 Prolene suture at the non-right commissures. We continue with a second needle, counterclockwise from inside to outside and back around the cylinder until we reach the right-left commissures. As was done for the right sinus, suturing of the left sinus begins slightly to the right and at the bottom of the left coronary ostium. In a typical inclusion manner, this line progresses up to the neo-STJ and continues by inclusion of the non-coronary sinus in a continuous manner. Then we switch to the other needle and, as described earlier, we go directly from inside to outside and back around the cylinder, completing its reimplantation at the left-right commissures.
- 6 - Final aortic valve assessment, neo-STJ reconstruction and distal anastomosis (14:00)
	We perform a final assessment of the implanted aortic graft. Any bulging is strongly avoided. The aorta is divided completely at the level of the STJ. We construct a neo-STJ with a Dacron prosthesis similar in size to the implanted cylinder with a Dacron skirt wrapping it proximally.
	After a final assessment of the reimplanted cylinder, the Dacron prosthesis and the native aorta are anastomosed with 4-0 Prolene sutures.
- 7 - Postoperative assessment and magnetic resonance study (17:16)
	Postoperative transoesophageal echocardiography shows no paravalvular leak, a peak aortic gradient below 10 mmHg and high coaptation at the level of the aortic annulus. On cardiac magnetic resonance images, the linear flow is low.

The technique presented here embraces an aortic stentless graft implant and its stabilization within the aortic root, in fact stabilizing both neo-aortic root components. We see renewed interest in and more clinical investigations into stentless implant techniques due to the recommendation of stentless autografts for patients younger than 50 years of age \[5\]. BAV has been considered a relative contraindication for the Ross procedure, so techniques of root stabilization were needed. Similarly, stentless heterografts were proven to stabilize against root dilatation when implanted for BAV with root disease. The resolution of post-stenotic dilatation was observed in which stentless heterograft implants were proven to counteract aneurysm formation in patients with BAV disease \[6\]. The presented technique can be universally used with both autografts and heterografts. There is no need to dissect coronaries, which significantly reduces early risk. In my institute, we have implanted 500 stentless grafts, with proven superior results \[7,8,9\]. The neo-aortic root technique described here may help in cases with complex surgical indications \[10\]. During 2 years of echocardiographic follow-up of our patient, we have observed stable haemodynamic results with no aortic regurgitation. The mean transaortic gradient decreased further to 3.08 mmHg from 10.7 mmHg early postoperatively. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging confirmed a linear flow profile with a velocity of 1.1 m/s.
