In this video tutorial we demonstrate our technique of root reinforcement of the pulmonary autograft in a Ross operation. This technique is being used with increasing frequently to prevent the formation of an aneurysm of the pulmonary autograft.

The patient presented in the video has a bicuspid aortic valve with severe symptomatic aortic valve stenosis and moderate regurgitation. After harvesting, the pulmonary autograft it is sized and a 3- or 5-mm oversized Valsalva vascular prosthesis is chosen for reinforcement and to prevent formation of an aneurysm. Sizing depends on the amount and thickness of muscle in the pulmonary autograft.

Our patient is a 30-year-old male with a bicuspid aortic valve, severe symptomatic aortic valve stenosis, and moderate regurgitation. The preoperative transthoracic echocardiography revealed a mean gradient of 45 mmHg, max gradient of 70 mmHg, and an AVA of 0.6 cm <sup>2</sup>. Left ventricular function is normal. Besides a history of arterial hypertension the patient has no comorbidities.

The Ross operation was chosen due to the young age of the patient and its advantages of durability without need for anticoagulation.

- 1 - Harvesting the pulmonary autograft (0:28)
	The main pulmonary artery is divided just proximal to its bifurcation and the pulmonary valve is examined. A right-angle clamp is passed through the pulmonary valve into the right ventricular infundibulum to guide the initial incision to the anterior wall, keeping a safe distance from the pulmonary valve.  
	  
	The incision is continued in both directions with great care being taken at the leftward extent, which is close to the first septal perforator branch of the left coronary artery. For the root-reinforced Ross operation we aim to harvest at least 5 mm of muscular tissue under the aortic valve to facilitate reimplantation into a sinus Valsalva prosthesis.
- 2 - Mobilization of the coronary arteries (1:40)
	The coronary arteries are mobilized, with buttons of aortic wall attached in a typical fashion.
- 3 - Excision of the aortic valve leaflets (1:50)
	The aortic valve is excised in standard fashion; the aortic valve annulus is preserved and any calcium deposits in the aortic annulus and the subannular structures are meticulously removed. A Hegar dilator is used to measure the aortic valve diameter.
- 4 - Pulmonary autograft preparation (2:14)
	Before implantation into a Valsalva vascular prosthesis, the pulmonary autograft is trimmed and excessive tissue that could narrow the autograft after implantation into the prosthesis is removed. The diameter of the pulmonary autograft is then measured with a Hegar dilator, taking care not to overstretch the autograft. Depending on the thickness of the autograft, a 3- or 5-mm oversized Valsalva vascular prosthesis (Vascutek, Renfrewshire, Scotland, UK) is then selected for reinforcement of the autograft.
	For implantation of the autograft into the Valsalva prosthesis, all 3 commissures of the autograft are tucked to the 120° marks of the prosthesis using a 5.0 polypropylene suture, in order to achieve optimal geometrical alignment, and a running 5-0 polypropylene suture is then run around the proximal end of the autograft. The distal end of the autograft is treated in an identical fashion.
- 5 - Pulmonary autograft reimplantation (5:30)
	Correct geometrical reimplantation of the autograft in a 120-120-120° alignment is ensured with a standard valve probe. In case of bicuspid valves or asymmetrical aortic sinuses, “neocommisures” are marked. Using these guiding points, the autograft is then implanted into the aortic annulus using a 4-0 running polypropylene suture.
- 6 - Coronary artery reimplantation (6:48)
	The coronary arteries are implanted into the autograft. In the area of the re-implantation of the coronary arteries, generous parts of the Valsalva prosthesis are removed. The coronary arteries are then re-implanted in a standard fashion using a 5-0 polypropylene suture.
- 7 - Implantation of a cryopreserved pulmonary homograft (9:19)
	The cryopreserved pulmonary homograft is implanted in standard fashion. We routinely re-implant the homograft prior to construction of the distal aortic anastomosis in order to optimize exposure of the pulmonary artery bifurcation.
- 8 -- Postoperative echocardiography (9:41)
	Postoperative transesophageal echocardiographic results show a competent autograft function as well as a competent homograft in pulmonary position.

The total time of cardiopulmonary bypass was 2 hours and 39 minutes including an aortic cross clamp time of 2 hours and 9 minutes. Buckberg cardioplegia solution was applicated antegrade by an aortic root cardioplegia cannula and by direct cardioplegia delivery into the coronary ostia.

The root-reinforced Ross operation prevents dilation of the pulmonary autograft and therefore prolongs the durability and competence of the valve’s function.
