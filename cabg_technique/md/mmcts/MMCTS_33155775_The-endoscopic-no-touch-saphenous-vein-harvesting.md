## Abstract

The no-touch saphenous vein harvesting technique is being increasingly used; however, this technique causes more leg wound complications than conventional techniques. Endoscopic saphenous vein harvesting is considered a safe and effective approach for reducing leg complications, despite the fact that experience with this technique remains limited, because leg CO2 insufflation and dissection with a tip cannula to isolate the vein enables the graft to naturally skeletonize.


In this video tutorial, we demonstrate our endoscopic no-touch saphenous vein harvesting technique using a reusable saphenous vein retractor system without CO2 insufflation and an electrothermal bipolar vessel sealing device.

## Introduction

No-touch saphenous vein (NT-SV) technique, which leaves a pedicle of perivascular tissue intact, preserving the outer vessel wall layers, is increasingly being used [1]; however, the open harvesting technique for NT-SV creates more wound complications than conventional techniques [1-3]. Experience with endoscopic NT-SV harvesting remains limited because leg CO2 insufflation and dissection with a tip cannula to isolate the vein, according to the previously reported technique, enables the graft to naturally skeletonize [4]. 


In our demonstration we introduce a reusable SV retractor system without CO2 sealing (RSRWC) (Karl Storz, Tuttlingen, Germany) using an electrothermal bipolar vessel sealing device (EBVSD) (Ligasure; Covidien, Boulder, CO, USA), with favorable outcomes.


Our strategy for triple-vessel coronary artery disease includes harvesting skeletonized internal mammary arteries for left coronary territory and no-touch saphenous veins for the obtuse marginal, posterolateral branches or posterior descending artery.


Exclusion criteria for this technique were emergent cases, those deemed with pre-ultrasonography evaluation to be inappropriate due to varicose or very small veins, and those with severe peripheral vascular disease.

## Outcome and discussion

Most leg wound complications following SV harvesting are caused by thermal injury from monopolar electrocautery and superficial large lymph vessel damage during sharp dissection [6]. We introduced EBVSD as this technique allows firm coagulation with minimal thermal spread to accurately seal blood and lymphatic vessels [7]. 


Endoscopic SV harvesting technique might reduce complications by decreasing the incision length, tissue trauma, and skin flaps, and some centers prefer endoscopic or bridge incision techniques for SV harvesting [8], due to the low harvest site complication rate. However, these veins may have lower patency rates [9]. 


Poor graft quality is attributed to structural injury [8]. To prevent injury and to allow superior exposure, this novel harvesting technique using the RSRWC, with an EBVSD, has the potential advantages of minimally-invasive endoscopic approach and the improved patency of NT-SV.

## Summary

No-touch saphenous vein harvesting technique using a reusable saphenous vein retractor system with an electrothermal bipolar vessel sealing device.
