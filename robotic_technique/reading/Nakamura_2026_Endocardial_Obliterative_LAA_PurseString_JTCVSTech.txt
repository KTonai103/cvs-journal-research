Endocardial obliterative left atrial appendage closure by
inverted purse-string plication
Yoshitsugu Nakamura, MD, PhD, Taisuke Nakayama, MD, PhD, Miho Kuroda, MD, Yuto Yasumoto, MD,
and Chihiro Shirai, ME
ABSTRACT
Objective: To describe an endocardial obliterative left atrial appendage closure
(LAAC) technique using inverted appendage purse-string plication added to 2-layer
orifice closure and to evaluate anatomic and clinical outcomes in minimally invasive
and robotic mitral surgery.
Methods: This retrospective study included consecutive patients who underwent
robotic mitral valve surgery, or atrial septal defect repair with tricuspid intervention,
with concomitant endocardial obliterative LAAC between April 2024 and June 2025.
The technique inverts the distal half of the left atrial appendage (LAA), compacts it
with a 3-0 barbed purse-string along an oblique line in the pectinate muscle, restores
native orientation, then performs a 2-layer orifice closure with polypropylene sutures.
Pre- and postoperative electrocardiogram-gated cardiac computed tomography
(ECG-CT) was used to assess anatomy. The primary end point was complete closure,
defined as absence of contrast filling within the LAA body in early and delayed phases.
Analyses were descriptive.
Results: Twenty-four patients (median age, 71.5 years) underwent endocardial
obliterative LAAC with the da Vinci system without conversion or intraoperative
bleeding requiring intervention. There were no perioperative deaths, strokes,
myocardial ischemic events, re-explorations, or left circumflex artery injuries. At
3 to 6 months, ECG-CT in all patients showed complete closure with no contrast
inflow, residual lumen, pericardial effusion, and periappendage hematoma.
Conclusions: Endocardial obliterative LAAC using inverted appendage pursestring plication followed by 2-layer orifice closure is a simple, inexpensive, and
safe adjunct to minimally invasive mitral surgery, achieving ECG-CT–confirmed
complete anatomic closure in this early series and potentially enhancing the effectiveness of surgical LAAC. (JTCVS Techniques 2026;36:102268)

PERSPECTIVE
Surgical LAAC performed concomitantly with
cardiac surgery reduces long-term stroke risk.
However, standard endocardial orifice-only
closure can leave a residual stump or small
perfused cavity on ECG-CT. An endocardial obliterative technique using inverted appendage
purse-string plication added to routine orifice
closure targets this failure geometry without
added invasiveness or device cost.

Concomitant left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) during cardiac surgery lowers the risk of ischemic stroke compared with
surgery alone.1 Endocardial LAAC with orifice closure from
the left atrium is attractive because access is straightforward
and the approach is economical. Its limitation is incomplete
closure, which is detected by electrocardiogram-gated cardiac
computed tomography (ECG-CT) in roughly one quarter of
cases.2 In our robot-assisted mitral program, double-layer
entrance suturing produced a similar ∼24% rate of late opacification on ECG-CT, typically as diffuse contrast filling of the
appendage from minute suture-line leaks—the so-called
“speech-bubble” sign.3
These observations led to a simple principle: rather than
merely closing the orifice, we should achieve luminal obliteration by inverting the distal half of the left atrial appendage
(LAA) and securing it with purse-string plication. We report

METHODS
Study Design and Patient Population
This was a single-center, retrospective observational study
conducted at Chibanishi General Hospital. We identified all
consecutive adult patients who underwent minimally invasive
or robotic mitral valve surgery with concomitant endocardial
LAAC using the inverted purse-string obliterative technique
between April 2024 and June 2025. The study was approved
by the institutional review board of Chibanishi General
Hospital (TGE02656-025) on November 5, 2024, which
waived the requirement for individual written informed
consent for this retrospective analysis of anonymized data.
Patient Selection and Exclusion Criteria
Patients were eligible if (1) they had a preoperative diagnosis of paroxysmal or chronic atrial fibrillation (AF) and
an indication for surgical mitral valve intervention or atrial
septal defect repair and tricuspid valve intervention and (2)
if they were scheduled to undergo concomitant obliterative
LAAC using the inverted purse-string obliterative technique. Demographic, echocardiographic, and operative variables were obtained from institutional cardiac surgery and
imaging databases.
Imaging Protocol
Preoperative ECG-CT was used to quantify left atrial/LAA
volumes, ostial size, morphology, and coronary proximity (Philips IntelliSpace Portal; slice thickness 0.8 mm, increment
0.4 mm; 75% cardiac phase). At 3 to 6 months, contrastenhanced ECG-synchronized CTwas obtained unless contraindicated. Both early and delayed phases were reviewed. The
primary anatomical end point was complete closure of the
LAA on postoperative ECG-CT, defined as the absence of
any contrast filling within the LAA body in both early and
delayed phases. The “speech-bubble” sign was defined as
any focal or diffuse contrast pooling distal to an apparently
closed LAA orifice on delayed-phase images.3
Surgical Technique
The procedure was performed concomitantly with mitral
surgery through a right thoracic robotic approach in most
cases. Cannulation and myocardial protection followed

institutional routine. A 3-0 barbed suture (V-Loc,
Medtronic plc) was used for plication and 3-0 and 4-0 polypropylene (PROLENE; Ethicon) were used for orifice
closure. The procedure for obliterative LAAC is shown in
Video 1 and Figure 1:
1. Inversion (Figure 1, B). Gently invert the LAA into the
left atrium with atraumatic forceps, creating an intraluminal oblique cone that is shorter anteriorly and longer
posteriorly.
2. Purse-string plication (Figure 1, C). Identify the
midpoint of the short anterior aspect of the inverted
cone and plan an oblique line toward the base. Obliquity
facilitates “tucking” the distal cone into the proximal
portion. As a rule of thumb, choose the line so that tissue
volumes distal and proximal to it are roughly balanced.
(Skin marking is optional and used only for demonstration in the video.) Along this line, place a purse-string
with 3-0 barbed suture, taking partial-thickness bites
confined to the pectinate muscle to reduce bleeding
risk. Avoid full-thickness passes. Tighten sequentially
to compact the distal cone.
3. Restore native orientation (Figure 1, D). Revert the LAA
toward its anatomic position. The proximal half returns
to native orientation, whereas the distal half remains inverted and secured by the purse-string, achieving
luminal obliteration.
4. Two-layer orifice closure (Figure 1, E). Close the orifice
with a horizontal-mattress layer followed by an overand-over running layer using 4-0 polypropylene. At the
ceiling of the orifice and at curvature changes, add a
short reinforcing bite if in doubt. (In the video, we
confirm from the epicardial side that the LAA shows a
gentle concavity without distortion.)

Keep bites in the pectinate muscle to limit bleeding.
Anticipate potential leak “hot spots” at the ceiling side of
the start point and at bends in the suture line. In tall, slender

RESULTS
Between April 2024 and June 2025, 24 consecutive
patients with AF associated with mitral valve disease or
with atrial septal defect and tricuspid regurgitation underwent obliterative LAAC using the da Vinci surgical system
(Intuitive Surgical, Inc). No adverse events—including
bleeding, arrhythmia, myocardial ischemia, cardiac or
noncardiac injury, stroke, or death—occurred. Notably, no
left circumflex artery injury was observed during LAA
closure.
All patients underwent postoperative ECG-CT. Demographics are summarized in Table 1. Median age was
71.5 years, 33% of patients were female, and the median
body surface area was 1.58 m2. In total, 25% had mild
chronic kidney disease, but no patients were on dialysis.
AF type was paroxysmal in 17% and long-standing persistent in 83%. Median CHADS2 and CHA2DS2-VASc scores
were 2 and 3, respectively. Median left ventricular ejection
fraction was 61.5% and left atrial diameter 49.8 mm. In total, 92% had moderate-to-severe or severe mitral regurgitation and 46% had moderate-to-severe tricuspid
regurgitation.
LAA anatomy is summarized in Table 2: cactus, 17%;
chicken-wing, 63%; windsock, 13%; and cauliflower,
8.3%. Mean LAA width was 31.9 mm, and mean LAA
volume 16.3 mL.

Operative details are shown in Table 3. A concomitant
maze procedure is generally performed when the F-wave
amplitude in lead V1 is ≥0.1 mV; conversely, the maze is
avoided when the F wave is nearly flat and/or the ventricular
rate is<50 beats per minute. In this series, 19 patients (79%)
underwent a concomitant maze procedure according to the
aforementioned institutional criteria. Obliterative LAAC
did not interfere with intracardiac procedures including
mitral valve surgery or maze procedure. No intraoperative
bleeding requiring intervention occurred. Intraoperative
transesophageal echocardiogram confirmed complete
closure of LAA at the end of the surgery. There was no mortality, no stroke, and no bleeding event during the follow-up
period (Table 4).
ECG-CT Assessment
All patients demonstrated complete closure with no contrast
inflow into the LAA on delayed ECG-CT (Table 4 and
Figure 2). No pericardial effusion or periappendage hematoma
was seen. No injuries to adjacent structures, including the left
coronary system, were identified.
DISCUSSION
Orifice-only endocardial closure can fail when a minute
defect permits delayed contrast entry, diffusely reexpanding
a residual lumen. By eliminating the intraluminal potential
space, the obliterative technique ensures that—even if
microscopic porosity remains at the orifice—there is no
cavity to opacify, thereby preventing suture-line leak. The
added step—inversion with purse-string plication—is simple. A further strength of this study is the systematic use
of ECG-CT to confirm anatomic completeness, providing
objective imaging validation beyond intraoperative

In the present series, obliterative endocardial LAAC
achieved complete closure irrespective of LAA morphology
or size. Using the Di Biase categories—cactus, chickenwing, windsock, and cauliflower—the distribution of morphologies appeared comparable with previous reports, supporting
the generalizability of the technique across typical anatomic
variants.6 Mechanistically, inversion with purse-string plication
obliterates the intraluminal potential space; completeness
therefore does not depend on a perfectly linear orifice seal.
Execution varies modestly with shape: when the inverted
cone is tall and elongated, the plication line can be less obliquity—often nearly parallel to the base—simplifying compaction without compromising completeness. Size metrics in
this series also fall within expected ranges, with reported
LAA volumes of ∼3.4 ± 1.1 mL/m2 in healthy controls7 and
∼22 ± 15 mL in AF cohorts8; our mean of 16.3 mL lies
squarely within the AF distribution. Taken together,

morphology and size chiefly inform where the oblique line is
planned and how deep the bites are taken in the pectinate
muscle, but they do not determine feasibility or the likelihood
of complete closure.
Large studies—most notably LAAOS III1—and several
surgical series support concomitant LAAC9,10 for stroke
prevention, but confirmation of anatomic completeness has
been rare. Endocardial entrance-suture techniques show
∼25% recanalization on ECG-CT.2,3 Because previous trials
demonstrated benefit without confirmed completeness, some
effect likely occurred despite unrecognized recanalization.
Future studies that compare CT-confirmed complete closure
versus no LAAC may therefore reveal a larger stroke reduction. Within surgical endocardial obliterative LAAC should
raise complete-closure rates and plausibly improve clinical
effectiveness in programs favoring endocardial techniques.
Study Limitations
This was a single-center early experience with modest
sample size; durability and clinical outcomes require longer
follow-up.
CONCLUSIONS
Obliterative LAAC—distal-half inversion with pursestring plication followed by 2-layer orifice closure—is
