We describe our technique for totally endoscopic, robotic-assisted thoracic and pericardial adhesiolysis and redo complex mitral valve repair.

A history of previous cardiothoracic surgery is often considered a relative contraindication to a minimally invasive approach for mitral valve surgery. However, adhesions can be approached safely and effectively by leveraging the magnified vision and the enhanced technical dexterity of the surgical robotic platform. We present our technique for totally endoscopic, robotic-assisted thoracic and pericardial adhesiolysis in patients with history of previous open or totally endoscopic cardiothoracic surgery. We also present our surgical technique as applied in a young patient with de novo, severe mitral regurgitation secondary to failure of a previous mitral valve repair due to partial annuloplasty ring dehiscence.

We present the case of a 32-year-old male with a history of mitral valve repair (34-mm annuloplasty ring, 3 Gore-Tex neochords (W.L. Gore, Newark, DE, USA) to the anterior leaflet), a previous Cox-maze procedure, and placement of a left atrial appendage epicardial clip performed at an outside institution. Twenty months after the first operation, the patient was referred to our center for severe mitral regurgitation. A preoperative transesophageal echocardiogram demonstrated partial dehiscence of the annuloplasty ring and 1 torn and 2 elongated Gore-Tex neochords. Infective endocarditis was excluded by appropriate investigations, and we offered a robotic-assisted, totally endoscopic approach for redo mitral valve repair. The operation was conducted with percutaneous femoral cannulation, replacement of the annuloplasty ring with a 36-mm annuloplasty band, placement of 1 Gore-Tex neochord to A1, 2 neochords to A2, and 1 neochord to A3.

- 1 - Introduction (0:12)
	The working port is placed in the third intercostal space at the anterior axillary line; the left robotic arm port is placed in the second intercostal space, halfway between the anterior axillary line and the midclavicular line; the right robotic arm port is placed in the fifth intercostal space, slightly below the anterior axillary line; and the left atrial retractor is placed in the fourth intercostal space, 2 cm medially to the midclavicular line. Of note, the ports of the 2 robotic arms and the camera port must be perfectly triangulated in order to maximize visualization of the right hemichest and the pericardial cavity. The ports must be inserted carefully and under direct visualization.
- 2 - First adhesiolysis case: Prior sternotomy (0:40)
	Our first case was a patient who had a previous midline sternotomy. Extensive adhesions were noted between the right lung and the right atrium. The dominant robotic arm was equipped with electrified curved scissors, and the nondominant robotic arm was equipped with surgical graspers. A combination of sharp and blunt adhesiolysis was performed with attention to always remaining within the cleavage planes. The articulating robotic instruments provided 7 degrees of freedom and allowed for tremor-free movements. Tissue traction and countertraction can be facilitated by the table-side assistant with long-shafted thoracoscopic forceps introduced through the working port. Previously placed suture lines and stitches can be used as landmarks of the anatomic structures, such as the site of reentry to the right atrium or to Waterston’s groove.
- 3 - Second adhesiolysis case: Prior sternotomy (2:17)
	Our second case was a patient with a history of a previous midline sternotomy. Interestingly, the disposition of the adhesion looked different than in the previous case. Nevertheless, the same technical principles were safely applied with the robotic instruments, and they allowed for complete adhesiolysis. Additional sutures were placed in this case on the pericardial folds with gentle traction to facilitate exposure. Again, previously placed suture lines were used as an anatomic guide. Finally, after the exposure of Waterston’s groove, the left atrium could be accessed.
- 4 - Third case: Prior robotic-assisted surgery (3:46)
	Our third case was a patient with a history of a previous robotic-assisted approach for mitral valve replacement. The same surgical principles were applied here, with great care taken to identify and avoid the phrenic nerve throughout the procedure.
- 5 - Fourth case: Complex repair technique (4:32)
	After inspecting the valve, the first step was careful removal of the previously placed annuloplasty ring. Neochordoplasty was then performed by placing 1 Gore-Tex neochord to A1, 2 neochords to A2, and 1 neochord to A3. As shown in the video, the robotic camera allows for optimal visualization of both the mitral valve and the subvalvular apparatus. The repair was completed by placement of a 36-mm annuloplasty band and by adjusting the height of the chords.

The patient had an uneventful postoperative course and was discharged home on postoperative day 4 with trace residual mitral regurgitation. In experienced hands, adhesions from previous cardiothoracic operations should not be considered an absolute contraindication to mitral valve surgery.

This case was presented at the AATS–Mitral Conclave Workshop (May 13-14, 2022) in Boston.
